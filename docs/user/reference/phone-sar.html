Phone SAR

A device or antenna placed against a flat phantom or a head, run at its bands, and psSAR1g and psSAR10g read at its rated power beside published reference values — shown as reference values, never as pass or fail.

A wireless-device engineer puts an antenna or a whole device against a phantom of tissue-simulating liquid, runs it at the device's bands, and reads psSAR1g and psSAR10g at the device's rated power. circuitRF does each step on an ordinary 3D view and an ordinary openEMS setup: the placement writes transforms, the band sweep writes setups, and the table reads their results.

Research and engineering use

circuitRF's SAR is for research and engineering. It is not a compliance tool: published limits are shown beside a result as reference values, and nothing says pass or fail.

The worked example is SAR: Phone Dipole (File ▸ New ▸ Examples): a system-check dipole under a flat phantom at 1.75 and 1.95 GHz. The phantoms are described in Tissues and phantoms, and SAR itself in SAR and B1.

1. A phantom

3D ▸ Draw ▸ Phantom:

  • Flat Phantom — a shell holding 150 mm of liquid, open on top; for a body-worn or flat position and for a system check. Set its liquid to the head or body tissue-simulating liquid.
  • Generic Head (circuitRF's own) — an ellipsoid shell of head liquid with a flat ear spacer on each side; for the position against the ear. It is circuitRF's own shape, not a standard's mannequin.

Both liquids are target tables, so each band's run evaluates the liquid at that band.

2. Placement

Select the antenna or the device — any objects, placed cells and groups — and 3D ▸ Draw ▸ Phantom ▸ Place Against Phantom… (or the canvas menu on the selection).

Field
Phantom, Face a phantom's own box faces (xmin … zmax), and its named ones: a flat phantom's floor (the shell's outside) and liquid (the liquid's surface through the shell), the generic head's ear-left and ear-right
Separation from the face's plane to the device's nearest point, measured on its geometry (a STEP part on its tessellation)
Tilt (°) about a turn about a world axis, applied first; the move is then solved
Offset x, y, z where the device's box sits on the face, from the face's centre

It moves the selection as one rigid body and writes ordinary transforms, what a drag would write: nothing in the file remembers the placement, and moving the device by hand afterwards is fine. The 3D view shows the separation as a dimension, measured again from the geometry whenever the view changes. Refused: placing a phantom itself; a negative separation; a separation from liquid thinner than the shell (the device would be inside it); an object whose position is an expression (placing it would overwrite the expression).

A tilt that is not a quarter turn is for a device made of solids. openEMS cannot take a sheet tilted out of every axis plane, or a lumped port pointing off the axes, so a drawn strip antenna fed by a lumped port refuses such a tilt before the run.

3. Bands and power

One run gives SAR at one frequency (the tissues are evaluated there). 3D ▸ Draw ▸ Phantom ▸ Band Sweep… makes one setup per band from a template:

Field
Template setup an openEMS setup with a SAR group: the grid, the boundaries, the masses
Bands (GHz) the frequencies
Rated power (W) the device's rated conducted power (default 1 W, so the table reads per watt), referred to the accepted (the power entering the antenna), or incident (the power the transmitter delivers into 50 Ω, of which the antenna's mismatch reflects |S11|²)
Duty cycle for a transmitter that sends in bursts: its scaling factor
Reference values for head and trunk, or limbs: which published values are shown
Your own values, Targets reference values of your own, and target values for a known configuration

Create Setups writes one setup per band, <sweep> <f> GHz: the template with its SAR at that band, 1 g and 10 g added to its masses, and its sweep scaled by band / template frequency, so each band keeps the template's relative bandwidth and a grid drawn from its own highest frequency. Creating again replaces them, so an edited template brings every band up to date. Run creates them and runs each in turn, as Simulate would; the dialog is not modal, because the runs take as long as they take. Power, duty cycle, reference values and targets are read by the table alone, so changing them never makes a result out of date.

4. The table

One row per band and driven port: psSAR1g and psSAR10g at the rated power, and per watt; the peak cube's centre and tissue; and the difference from a target where you gave one. A band not run yet says so.

Published reference values are shown beside each number, each with its source and date:

Reference value Source
1.6 W/kg over 1 g, head and trunk FCC OET Bulletin 65 Supplement C (Edition 01-01, June 2001), general population, partial body
4.0 W/kg over 10 g, extremities the same table
2.0 W/kg over 10 g, head and trunk ICNIRP Guidelines (100 kHz to 300 GHz), Health Phys. 118(5):483–524 (2020), general public, local
4.0 W/kg over 10 g, limbs the same

A number above one is coloured and says which reference value it is above.

Headless, circuitrf em view.c3d --bands <sweep> makes the same setups in memory, runs them and prints the table; MCP's run analysis=em takes bands.

A system check

A laboratory checks its measurement system with a half-wave dipole at a stated spacing below a flat phantom, and compares the measured psSAR1g with a target value for that dipole. The example reproduces the configuration the FCC bulletin gives for 1900 MHz — a 68.0 mm dipole, 10.0 mm from its axis to the liquid, a 2.0 mm shell, 15 cm of head liquid — and its results are circuitRF's own, not target values: the target values come from a standard that is not reproduced here. If you have them, enter them as the sweep's targets.

At 1.9 GHz, per watt accepted, the same check on three grids:

CellsPerWavelength 10 15 20
cells 0.87 M 2.30 M 4.22 M
psSAR1g 39.08 W/kg 40.87 W/kg 40.21 W/kg
psSAR10g 20.00 W/kg 20.97 W/kg 20.78 W/kg

From 15 cells per wavelength on, psSAR agrees with the finest grid to 2 %; at 10 it reads 3–4 % low. The approach is not monotonic, because each grid places the dipole's edges and feed differently and the dipole's own resonance moves with them.

What is not here

Hand and body-worn positions with a hand phantom, absorbed power density above 6 GHz, and compliance reports.
